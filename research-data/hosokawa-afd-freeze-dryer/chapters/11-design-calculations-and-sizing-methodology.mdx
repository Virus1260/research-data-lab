# 11: Design Calculations & Sizing Methodology

All worked calculations below evaluate a representative **10 kg batch (10 L liquid) in a 20 L nominal vessel**. This batch matches Hosokawa's published 20 L Active Freeze Dryer model (10 L batch capacity, 0.8 kg/h sublimation capacity). This allows direct cross-checking of first-principles physics against commercial performance figures.

## 1. Sublimation rate and thermal duty

The core sublimation energy equation is:

$$Q = \dot{m} \cdot \Delta H_s$$

For ice, latent heat of sublimation is $\Delta H_s \approx 2{,}838\text{ kJ/kg}$.

Hosokawa specifies $\dot{m} = 0.8\text{ kg/h}$ for the 20 L model:

$$\dot{m} = \frac{0.8\text{ kg}}{3600\text{ s}} = 2.222 \times 10^{-4}\text{ kg/s}$$

$$Q = 2.222 \times 10^{-4}\text{ kg/s} \times 2{,}838{,}000\text{ J/kg} \approx 631\text{ W}$$

A thermal heat duty of 631 W sustains 0.8 kg/h sublimation. This thermal load is compact and readily delivered by an electric circulation TCU.

For a 10 kg batch with 90% water content (9.0 kg ice), primary drying time calculates as:

$$t_{\text{drying}} = \frac{9.0\text{ kg}}{0.8\text{ kg/h}} \approx 11.25\text{ hours}$$

This drying duration aligns with Hosokawa's patent range of 10 to 100 hours for industrial batches.

## 2. Required jacket heat-transfer area

Compute heat exchange surface using:

$$Q = U \cdot A \cdot \Delta T \implies A = \frac{Q}{U \cdot \Delta T}$$

- $U$: Overall heat transfer coefficient from jacket fluid to the agitated granular bed. For an actively stirred bed under vacuum, plan for $50\text{ to }200\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$. Agitation eliminates the stagnant gas gap found in static vial freeze drying.
- $\Delta T$: Temperature difference between the jacket wall and the sublimation front (typically 10 to 30 K).

At $Q = 631\text{ W}$:

| Assumed $U$ ($\text{W/m}^2\text{K}$) | Required Area $A$ ($\text{m}^2$) at $\Delta T = 20\text{ K}$ |
|---|---|
| 50 | 0.63 |
| **100** | **0.32** |
| 150 | 0.21 |
| 200 | 0.16 |

At $U = 100\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$, required area is $0.32\text{ m}^2$. Sizing a 20 L cone with a 25° half-angle yields exactly $0.32\text{ m}^2$ of internal wetted lateral area.

## 3. Freezing-stage refrigeration duty

Cooling a 10 kg aqueous charge (9.0 kg water, 1.0 kg solids) from +20 °C to -30 °C requires three thermal steps:

$$Q_1 = m \cdot c_{p,\text{liq}} \cdot \Delta T_1 = 10\text{ kg} \times 4.2\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K} \times 20\text{ K} = 840\text{ kJ}$$

$$Q_2 = m_{\text{water}} \cdot \Delta H_f = 9\text{ kg} \times 334\text{ kJ/kg} = 3{,}006\text{ kJ}$$

$$Q_3 = m \cdot c_{p,\text{ice}} \cdot \Delta T_2 = 10\text{ kg} \times 2.1\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K} \times 30\text{ K} = 630\text{ kJ}$$

$$\text{Total Thermal Removal } Q_{\text{total}} = 840 + 3{,}006 + 630 = 4{,}476\text{ kJ } (4.48\text{ MJ})$$

Hosokawa patents define optimum freezing rates between 0.1 °C/min and 10 °C/min:

| Target Freeze Time | Cooling Rate (50 °C delta) | Average Refrigeration Duty |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0 h | 0.42 °C/min | 0.62 kW |
| 1.0 h | 0.83 °C/min | 1.24 kW |
| 0.5 h | 1.67 °C/min | 2.49 kW |

Apply a 1.5x to 2.0x engineering factor to account for jacket thermal mass, ambient heat leak, and pump dissipation. Specify a 2.5 to 3.5 kW refrigeration unit.

## 4. Vacuum pump evacuation time

Dry chamber pump-down time follows the volumetric evacuation equation:

$$t = \left(\frac{V}{S}\right) \cdot \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right)$$

For a 20 L ($0.02\text{ m}^3$) dry vessel pumping from 1,013 mbar down to 1 mbar:

| Pump Displacement $S$ | Calculated Evacuation Time |
|---|---|
| 5 $\text{m}^3/\text{h}$ | ~100 s |
| 10 $\text{m}^3/\text{h}$ | ~50 s |
| 20 $\text{m}^3/\text{h}$ | ~25 s |
| 50 $\text{m}^3/\text{h}$ | ~10 s |

This calculation covers initial dry chamber drawdown only. During sublimation, water vapor volumetric flow dominates. At 1 mbar and -20 °C, 0.8 kg/h of vapor equates to over 250 L/s of volumetric gas flow. The cold condenser traps over 95% of this vapor, protecting the dry vacuum pump.

## 5. Agitator motor sizing

Granular agitated bed rheology under freezing vacuum cannot be modeled accurately from first principles alone.
- Hosokawa's 500 L Nauta industrial mixer operates with a 2.2 kW drive.
- A 20 L freeze dryer requires significantly less power (100 W to 250 W continuous).
- Size the drive motor for peak torque during the slush-to-ice phase inversion. Equip the drive with a variable frequency controller (VFD) and monitor motor current continuously.

## 6. External-pressure shell thickness: ASME UG-28

The vessel operates under external vacuum pressure (1.013 bar differential):
- Thin conical shells fail by elastic buckling rather than plastic yielding.
- Sizing follows ASME Section VIII Division 1, paragraph UG-28.
- The engineer assumes an initial wall thickness $t$, computes diameter-to-thickness ($D_o/t$) and length-to-diameter ($L/D_o$) ratios, and retrieves Strain Factor $A$ from Section II Part D Subpart 3 Figure G.
- Using Factor $A$ and design metal temperature, read Stress Factor $B$ from material charts.
- Allowable external working pressure computes as:

$$P_a = \frac{4 \cdot B}{3 \cdot (D_o/t)}$$

Verify that $P_a \ge 1.033\text{ bar}$ plus your required safety margin.

## 7. External pressure tooling methodology: Division 1 vs Division 2 vs PV Elite

Understanding the calculation methodology determines software implementation:

1. **ASME Section VIII Division 1 (UG-28)**:
   - Uses empirical look-up charts. Factor $A$ comes from Figure G. Factor $B$ derives from material stress curves in Section II Part D.
   - These curves represent proprietary ASME compiled data. Do not use AI models to guess curve values.
2. **ASME Section VIII Division 2 (Part 4.4 - Miller Method)**:
   - Formulated as closed-form algebraic equations.
   - Eliminates chart lookups.
   - Readily programmed into automated computational tools.
3. **PV Elite and Commercial Pressure Vessel Solvers**:
   - Industry-standard validated solver for ASME certified code calculations.
   - Integrates ASME material databases and safety factors.
4. **Interactive Vessel Sizing Suite (Built in this Lab)**:
   - Provides preliminary sizing, live geometric exploration, and nozzle coordination.
   - Implements the open, published **Windenburg-Trilling equation** for classical elastic buckling:

$$P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{2.42 \cdot E \cdot (t / D_o)^{5/2}}{(1 - \nu^2)^{3/4} \cdot \left[\frac{L}{D_o} - 0.45 \cdot (t / D_o)^{1/2}\right]}$$

   - Applying a structural safety factor of 3.0 gives the preliminary allowable pressure:

$$P_{\text{allowable}} = \frac{P_{\text{cr}}}{3.0}$$

**Fabrication Rule**: Use the Sizing Suite for rapid dimensional optimization and nozzle scheduling. Perform final certified thickness validation in PV Elite prior to cutting metal.
