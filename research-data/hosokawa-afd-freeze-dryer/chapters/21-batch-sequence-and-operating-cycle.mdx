# 21 — Batch Sequence & Operating Cycle (ISA-88 State Machine)

The traditional pitfall in bespoke automation projects is writing one long, monolithic script: *"step 1, then step 2, then step 3..."* That approach functions initially, but collapses when engineers need to alter hold times, qualify validation runs, or handle unexpected process alarms.

To build an industrial-grade, reproducible pharmaceutical freeze dryer, we follow **ANSI/ISA-88 (IEC 61512)** — the global benchmark for automated batch control.

## 1. The ISA-88 Hierarchical Model

ISA-88 establishes a strict four-layer hierarchy that isolates process intent from equipment execution:

```
PROCEDURE         (Master Batch Recipe: "Complete AFD Lyophilization")
   └── UNIT PROCEDURE   (Major Phase: "Freezing", "Drying", "Discharge", "CIP/SIP")
          └── OPERATION        (Discrete Goal: "Primary Sublimation Drying")
                 └── PHASE            (Atomic Reusable Unit: "Hold Chamber Vacuum")
```

### Why Phases Are the Magic Building Block
A **Phase** is the smallest self-contained, independently verifiable unit of control logic.
- A phase like `"Ramp Jacket Temperature"` does not know or care whether it is executing during an initial trial batch or a validated commercial lot.
- It receives clean recipe parameters (target temperature: `-15°C`, ramp rate: `0.5°C/min`, hold duration: `120 min`), executes its closed-loop PID control, and signals completion when its objective is fulfilled.

### Standard States & Behaviors
- **IDLE**: The phase is initialized, waiting for the supervisory sequencer to provide setpoint parameters and trigger the `Start` command.
- **RUNNING**: The phase actively drives its actuators (e.g. `TIC-201` modulates `FCV-201`, or `SIC-101` turns `M-101`).
- **HELD**: Process is paused safely (e.g. operator paused the batch to inspect a filter). Actuators hold safe steady-state values.
- **ABORTING / ABORTED**: An interlock or critical fault occurred (e.g. vacuum loss or refrigeration trip). The phase immediately de-energizes heaters, closes isolation valves, and hands control over to emergency shutdown routines.

## 4. Automated Phase Transition: Comparative Pressure Convergence

In traditional freeze dryers, operators guess the end of primary drying using fixed timers, risking either unfinished batches or wasted machine hours.

In this automated AFD architecture, the transition from **Primary Drying** to **Secondary Drying** is triggered automatically:
1. `PIT-101A` (Capacitance Manometer) reads absolute total pressure.
2. `PIT-101B` (Pirani Sensor) reads apparent pressure skewed high by water vapor thermal conductivity.
3. While active sublimation occurs, `PIT-101B` exceeds `PIT-101A` by up to 60%.
4. As sublimation nears completion, water vapor diminishes, replaced by background nitrogen bleed.
5. When the ratio:
   $$\Delta P = \frac{P_{\text{Pirani}} - P_{\text{Capacitance}}}{P_{\text{Capacitance}}} \le 0.05$$
   remains true for a continuous 20-minute window, the PLC automatically transitions the batch from Primary to Secondary Drying.
