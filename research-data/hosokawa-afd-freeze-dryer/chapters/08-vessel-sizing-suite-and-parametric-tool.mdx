# 08 — Parametric Vessel Sizing Suite & Engineering Tools

This specification formalizes the physical models from Chapter 05 §1a/§1b and Chapter 11 §6-7 into an operational, interactive mechanical sizing architecture: a parametric conical freeze-dryer suite matching Hosokawa's published 10-model industrial spectrum (1 L to 1,500 L nominal).

## 1. What this tool is and what it is not

**It is**: a real-time, interactive engineering explorer for conical freeze-dryer vessel geometry across the full industrial size range (1 L to 1,500 L nominal). It establishes dynamic coupling between batch working volume, cone half-angle ($\alpha = 20^\circ$ to $35^\circ$), top major diameter ($D_{\text{major}}$), apex minor diameter ($D_{\text{minor}}$), height stack, lateral transfer area, 50 mm annular jacket hydrodynamics, ASME Section VIII UG-32 formed heads, and elastic vacuum buckling stability. It exports dimensionally certified nozzle schedules directly for CAD modeling and P&ID integration (Chapter 24).

**It is not**: a substitute for certified ASME BPVC Section VIII Division 1 or Division 2 stress calculations performed in licensed software such as PV Elite. Chapter 11 §7 codifies this clear boundary: this sizing suite generates preliminary mechanical architecture, geometric envelopes, and thermal feasibility bounds. Final nozzle reinforcement pads, knuckle high-stress concentrations, seismic anchoring, and certified full-vacuum buckling margins must be stamped in PV Elite prior to cutting metal.

### 2.1 Conical Shell Geometry Workbench (Height, Diameter, Slant & Lateral Area)

In a conical vessel, volume $V$ is governed by total height $h$ and half-angle $\alpha$:
$$V = \frac{\pi}{3} \cdot \tan^2(\alpha) \cdot h^3 \implies h = \left[\frac{3 \cdot V}{\pi \cdot \tan^2(\alpha)}\right]^{1/3}$$

### 2.2 The Non-Linear Cone Fill Height Insight (V ∝ h³)

In a conical dryer, volume scales with the **cube** of vertical height. While a cylindrical vessel fills linearly ($V \propto h$), a cone concentrates volume at the top. Filling a conical vessel to 50% working volume raises the liquid surface to:
$$h_{\text{fill}} = h \cdot (0.5)^{1/3} \approx 0.794 \cdot h \quad (79.4\%\text{ of total height})$$

This leaves only $20.6\%$ linear freeboard. Attempting to fill to $80\%$ volume pushes the liquid surface to $92.8\%$ of total height (leaving only $7.2\%$ freeboard), suffocating the orbiting orbital screw agitator, blocking subliming vapor escape paths, and triggering severe dry cake entrainment into the top vacuum filter plenum.

### 2.3 Sublimation Heat Duty & Annular Heat-Transfer Area Cross-Check

The sublimation process requires continuous latent heat input through the conical jacketed wall ($Q = \dot{m} \cdot \Delta H_s$). The jacket surface area must satisfy:
$$A_{\text{req}} = \frac{Q}{U \cdot \Delta T}$$

If the conical lateral surface area $A_{\text{lat}}$ is insufficient to transfer this thermal load, the vessel design requires a straight cylindrical shell extension at the top to close the thermal deficit. Explore the trade-offs below:

### 2.4 ASME Section VIII UG-32 Dished Head Thickness Calculator

The top vessel closure must withstand internal clean steam-in-place (SIP) pressure up to 3.0 to 3.5 barg at 135°C under ASME BPVC Section VIII Division 1 UG-32 rules:
- **Torispherical (ASME Flanged & Dished)**: $L = D$, $r = 0.06D$, stress coefficient $M = \frac{3 + \sqrt{L/r}}{4} \approx 1.77$:
  $$t = \frac{P \cdot L \cdot M}{2SE - 0.2P}$$
- **2:1 Semi-Ellipsoidal**: $L = 0.9D$, $r = 0.17D$, stress coefficient $K = 1.0$:
  $$t = \frac{P \cdot D \cdot K}{2SE - 0.2P}$$
- **Flat Bolted Blind Flange**: Structurally inefficient under vacuum; ASME guidelines restrict flat heads to diameters $\le 300\text{ mm}$.

::HeadThicknessWorkbench

### 2.5 Preliminary Vacuum Buckling & External Pressure Analysis

During freeze-drying, the vessel operates under deep vacuum (0.01 to 0.1 mbar) while the thermal fluid jacket operates at positive pressure (1.5 to 3.0 barg), producing severe net external collapsing pressure. Classical elastic shell buckling is governed by the Windenburg-Trilling formulation:
$$P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{2.42 \cdot E \cdot (t / D_o)^{5/2}}{(1 - \nu^2)^{3/4} \cdot \left[\frac{L}{D_o} - 0.45 \cdot (t / D_o)^{1/2}\right]}$$

With an ASME Section VIII design factor of safety $\text{FS} = 3.0$, allowable external vacuum pressure $P_{\text{allowable}} = P_{\text{cr}} / 3.0$ must strictly exceed atmospheric differential pressure (1.013 bar).

::BucklingPressureWorkbench

### 4.4 50 mm Annular Jacket Heat Transfer & Hydrodynamics Matrix

The annular space between the inner conical shell (product contact 316L) and the outer jacket shell (304L) is engineered with a **50 mm nominal radial gap** to achieve optimal hydrodynamic performance:

| Sizing Parameter | Engineering Value | Governing Physical Law | Engineering Rationale for 50 mm Annular Gap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominal Radial Gap ($\delta$)** | **50 mm** (Adjustable 20 to 100 mm) | Geometric radial clearance | A 50 mm gap maintains ideal thermal fluid velocity ($1.2\text{ to }1.8\text{ m/s}$) at standard TCU pump flow rates (30 to 80 LPM), guaranteeing fully developed turbulent convection ($Re > 4,000$) without excessive pressure drops. |
| **Inner Cone OD** | $D_{\text{major}} + 2 \cdot t_{\text{shell}}$ ($+8\text{ mm}$) | Product contact wall | 4.0 mm to 6.0 mm 316L (EN 1.4404) electropolished to $Ra \le 0.2\,\mu\text{m}$. |
| **Jacket Inner Diameter (ID)** | $\text{Cone OD} + 2 \cdot \delta$ ($+100\text{ mm}$ for 50 mm gap) | Annular envelope | The jacket inner wall is spaced exactly 50 mm radially from the cone outer wall. |
| **Jacket Outer Diameter (OD)** | $\text{Jacket ID} + 2 \cdot t_{\text{jacket}}$ ($+6\text{ mm}$) | Outer pressure containment | 3.0 mm to 5.0 mm 304L (EN 1.4307) outer structural jacket shell. |
| **Thermal Fluid Holdup** | $V_{\text{jkt}} \approx A_{\text{lat}} \cdot \delta$ (Liters) | Annular fluid volume | For AFD-20 with $A_{\text{lat}} = 0.267\text{ m²}$ and 50 mm gap, fluid holdup is $\approx 13.4\text{ L}$ ($12.3\text{ kg}$ Syltherm XLT), ensuring rapid dynamic temperature response. |
| **Inlet / Outlet Orientation** | Bottom Apex (J1 In) $\rightarrow$ Top Flange (J2 Out) | Natural convection and venting | Fluid enters at the lowest apex cone elevation (J1) and discharges at the top flange ring (J2) to continuously purge entrained air bubbles and prevent vapor locking. |

## 6. Architectural Role within the Research Lab

This sizing suite connects engineering theory from Chapter 05 (conical vessel mechanics and agitator kinematics) and Chapter 11 (thermodynamic sublimation balances and PV Elite code boundaries) directly into production artifacts:

1. **Chapter 13 (Engineering Drawings)**: Seeds dimensional elevations for general arrangement CAD drawings.
2. **Chapter 24 (CAD Nozzle Schedule)**: Feeds exact nozzle coordinates for process ports (N1 vacuum vapor, N2 product feed, N3 CIP sprayballs, N4 sampling port, N5 illumination sight glass, N6 vacuum release, N7 rupture disc, J1 jacket inlet, J2 jacket outlet, and bottom discharge port).
3. **P&ID Skid Automation**: Supplies fluid holdup volumes to the Temperature Control Unit (TCU) heat exchanger skid for cascade temperature loop tuning.
