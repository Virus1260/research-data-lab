# 15 — Complete Piping & Instrumentation Diagram (P&ID)

If P&IDs feel intimidating, it's usually because nobody first explained that **a P&ID is just a map with a very consistent alphabet.** Once you know the alphabet, any P&ID — this one, or a refinery unit twenty times more complex — reads the same way. This file teaches the alphabet, then gives you the actual diagram for this machine.

A machine-readable version of this piping and instrumentation architecture lives in `pid_controls_data/pid_data_model.json` (equipment, instruments, valves, and lines as structured data — see §5).

## 1. The alphabet: ISA 5.1 instrument tags

Every bubble on a P&ID has a short code in it, like `PIT-101A` or `TIC-201`. This is standardized by **ANSI/ISA-5.1** ("Instrumentation Symbols and Identification"), and it always decodes the same way:

<IsaTagAnatomyChart />

**First letter — what's being measured:**

| Letter | Measures |
|---|---|
| P | Pressure (or vacuum) |
| T | Temperature |
| F | Flow |
| L | Level |
| A | Analysis (composition — e.g. O₂ concentration) |
| S | Speed |
| J | Power / torque-related |
| Z | Position |

**Later letters — what the instrument *does*:**

| Letter | Function |
|---|---|
| T | Transmitter — measures continuously and sends a signal |
| I | Indicator — just displays a value locally |
| C | Controller — compares the measurement to a setpoint and calculates an output |
| S | Switch — trips at one threshold (not a continuous signal) |
| V | Valve |
| E | Element — the raw sensor itself (e.g. an RTD) |

So: `PIT-101A` = **P**ressure, **I**ndicating, **T**ransmitter, loop 101, unit "A" (because this machine deliberately has two pressure sensors on the same vessel — see §3). `TIC-201` = **T**emperature **I**ndicating **C**ontroller, loop 201. Once you can do this decoding, every tag on the diagram stops being a mystery symbol and starts being a sentence.

**Bubble shape also carries meaning:** a plain circle is a **field-mounted** instrument (a local gauge or sensor with no display in the control room). A circle with a horizontal line through it is a **control-system point** — its value lives in the PLC/HMI (the BPCS — see file 20). On this diagram, instruments with a **red** circle instead of black are **Safety Instrumented Functions (SIS)** — deliberately drawn differently because they live on a completely separate, independent safety system from everything else (file 20 explains why that separation matters so much).

## 2. Line types

- **Solid black line** — process piping (product, vapor, gas actually flowing).
- **Blue line** — utility piping (jacket heat-transfer fluid, refrigerant).
- **Red solid line** — clean steam (SIP), where fitted.
- **Grey dashed line** — an instrument signal going to the ordinary control system (BPCS).
- **Red dashed line** — an instrument signal going to the safety system (SIS) instead.

## 3. Walking the Diagram: Subsystem by Subsystem

Starting at the primary freeze-drying vessel (`V-101`, center) and working outward mirrors the physical process flow:

- **Jacket & TCU Loop (Left):** `TCU-201` circulates heat-transfer fluid (silicone oil) to the vessel's double jacket. `TT-201`/`TT-202` measure supply and return temperature; `TIC-201` compares supply temperature to recipe setpoints and modulates `FCV-201`. Cascade refrigeration package `PKG-301` supplies the low-temperature duty (down to $-60^\circ\text{C}$) for the freezing stage, while an electric/steam heat exchanger supplies sublimation enthalpy ($\Delta H_{\text{sub}} = 2,840\text{ kJ/kg}$).
- **Agitator & 10:1 Drive (Top of Vessel):** `M-101` drives the cantilevered auger screw (~70 rpm), while `M-102` powers the orbital sweep arm (1–2 rpm). `SIC-101` sets rotational speed from the BPCS. `JIT-101` continuously monitors auger motor torque and current draw — providing real-time indication of slurry viscosity and crystallization during vacuum freezing.
- **Dual Pressure Measurement (Comparative Endpoint):** Two independent vacuum sensors monitor the chamber: `PIT-101A` (capacitance manometer, gas-species independent true mechanical pressure) and `PIT-101B` (Pirani thermal conductivity gauge, reading ~1.6× high in water vapor atmospheres). `PIC-101` modulates throttle control valve `PV-102`. As primary sublimation completes and water vapor diminishes, the reading of `PIT-101B` converges toward `PIT-101A`, signaling the transition to secondary desorption.
- **Heated Filter Dome & Vapor Circuit (Right of Vessel):** Sublimed vapor exits through overhead dome `V-102`, heated to **+40 °C** via temperature loop `TIC-102` (`TT-102` / `HE-102`) to prevent vapor condensation on the filter textile. Differential pressure transmitter `PDT-102` monitors filter cake buildup. Pulse valve `XV-104` fires high-pressure dry nitrogen (4–6 bar) to dislodge powder cake. In-line throttle valve `PV-102` and bypass valve `XV-103` implement the dual-mode recovery disclosed in Hosokawa patent NL2026893B1.
- **Refrigerated Ice Condenser & Vacuum Train:** Downstream vapor enters ice condenser `E-101` (coils maintained at $-60^\circ\text{C}$ to $-75^\circ\text{C}$ by cascade skid `PKG-301`), freezing out the solvent vapor. Non-condensable gases are evacuated by vacuum pumping package `PKG-401` (dry screw pump with Roots booster).
- **Sanitary Bottom Discharge:** Finished powder drains through `XV-105`, a zero-dead-leg sanitary ball segment valve equipped with an inflatable silicone seat seal, discharging directly into a sealed transfer isolator.
- **Authentic Dual-Loop WIP/CIP System (Bottom):** Verified against Hosokawa CAD Drawings `8390096_1`, `8390105_1`, and `8390106_1`:
  - **Cover Spray Ring Loop:** `PIT-701` and flow valve `FV-701` deliver 6 bar @ 9 m³/hr to the 5 fixed spray nozzles spaced at **115 mm pitch** in the top dished cover, providing $360^\circ$ impingement over the orbital drive head, universal joint, and mechanical seal envelope.
  - **Cone Wall Wash Loop:** `PIT-702` and flow valve `FV-702` deliver 3 bar wash fluid to downward-angled nozzles wetting the steep $30^\circ$ cone wall for complete sheeting coverage.
- **Safety Instrumented Functions (SIS):** Passive spring-loaded vacuum relief valve `VRV-101` protects against accidental vessel pressurization. Safety switch `PSH-101` (high pressure trip) and door interlock `ZS-101` (lid closed confirmation) connect to an independent safety controller, enforcing fail-safe isolation independently of the BPCS.

## 4. Why Instrumentation Tags Share Loop Identifiers

Tags sharing a numerical identifier (such as `PIT-101A` and `PIT-101B`) indicate paired instruments monitoring the same process location for a shared analytical purpose. In the AFD, the difference $\Delta P = P_{\text{Pirani}} - P_{\text{Capacitance}}$ serves as the primary analytical indicator of sublimation phase completion.
