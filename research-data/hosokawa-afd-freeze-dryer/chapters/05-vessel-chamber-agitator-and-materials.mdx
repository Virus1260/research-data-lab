![Stirred Conical Freeze Dryer Schematic](/diagrams/stirred_conical_freeze_dryer_schematic.png)

## 1. Vessel geometry

Per NL1022668C2 and NL2026893B1 (file 03), the vessel features:
- **Downward-conical geometry** (cone point at bottom), optionally with a short cylindrical section at the top near the lid to give room for the drive, seals, and top-mounted nozzles. The patent explicitly notes this is common on classical conical mixers.
- A cylindrical vessel with only a conical bottom section is an acceptable, more space-efficient variant. The cylindrical-to-conical proportion must preserve mixing action.
- **Double-jacketed wall**: two concentric compartments (per NL2026893B1 Figure 1B) around the vessel wall. Each carries heat-exchange media, allowing wall cooling during freezing and heating during drying without fluid cross-contamination.
- **Open top**, closed by a top lid fixed with clamping screws. Quick-release clamp closures provide sanitary equivalents in workshop builds.
- **Open bottom**, closed by a releasable outlet (ball-segment valve) for complete discharge.

Hosokawa does not publish vessel diameter, height, wall thickness, or exact cone angle for the AFD. Treat vessel geometry as a design variable you calculate based on your target batch volume.

## 1a. Sizing the vessel: working-volume ratio, cone angle, and closures

![Vessel Sizing Geometry and Working Volume Relationship](/diagrams/vessel_sizing_geometry.png)

### The working-volume ratio is published data: 50%, not 80%

Hosokawa publishes a table of 10 nominal models from 1 L to 1,500 L. Every model specifies a maximum batch volume of exactly 50% nominal vessel volume:
- 1 L vessel holds 0.5 L batch.
- 20 L vessel holds 10 L batch.
- 100 L vessel holds 50 L batch.
- 1,500 L vessel holds 750 L batch.

This constant holds across two orders of magnitude. Use 50% as your design basis.

The AFD requires more headroom than an ordinary liquid tank for four physical reasons:
1. **The orbiting agitator needs headspace.** A Nauta-style screw lifts and folds product along the wall. Filling beyond 50% restricts product turnover at the top.
2. **The frozen bed occupies more volume than liquid.** Ice is 9% less dense than water. In addition, agitated freezing breaks the mass into loose, snow-like granules. Loose powder beds contain substantial gas voids. Total bulking frequently exceeds 30% to 50%. A 50% fill leaves a 2x volume safety factor. An 80% fill leaves only 1.25x margin.
3. **Vapor needs an unobstructed disengagement zone.** Sublimed water vapor travels from the bed to the top nozzle. Ample headspace reduces vapor superficial velocity, preventing fine powder carryover into the dust collector.
4. **Aqueous solutions foam under vacuum pull.** Dissolved gases boil out during initial evacuation, raising the liquid surface temporarily.

### Volume fraction versus height fraction in a cone

In a downward cone, volume scales with the cube of liquid height:

$$h_{\text{fill}} = h_{\text{total}} \cdot \left(\frac{V_{\text{fill}}}{V_{\text{nominal}}}\right)^{1/3}$$

| Volume Fill (%) | Liquid Height (% of Total) | Freeboard Height (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | 66.9% | 33.1% |
| 40% | 73.7% | 26.3% |
| **50%** | **79.4%** | **20.6%** |
| 60% | 84.3% | 15.7% |
| 70% | 88.8% | 11.2% |
| 80% | 92.8% | 7.2% |

At 50% volume fill, liquid reaches 79.4% of total cone height. You retain only 20.6% of vertical height as freeboard. At 80% volume fill, freeboard collapses to 7.2% of height.

To add physical headroom without widening the floor footprint, add a short cylindrical straight-side above the cone. Cylinder volume scales linearly with height, providing freeboard more efficiently than cone elongation.

### Selecting cone half-angle

Hosokawa Nauta mixer dimensions indicate half-angles between 20° and 22.5° from vertical. Practical builds operate between 20° and 35°:
- **Steeper cone (20°)**: Taller vessel with smaller top diameter. Improves gravity powder discharge. Maximizes external pressure buckling resistance by reducing unsupported shell span.
- **Shallower cone (35°)**: Shorter overall height. Larger top diameter increases buckling risk under vacuum. Leaves larger residual powder heels at the apex.

### Worked sizing example: 10 L working volume (20 L nominal vessel)

For a 10 L liquid batch, size a 20 L nominal vessel. Select a 25° half-angle ($\alpha = 25^\circ$):

$$V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{\pi}{3} \cdot \tan^2(\alpha) \cdot h^3 \implies h = \left[\frac{3 \cdot V}{\pi \cdot \tan^2(\alpha)}\right]^{1/3}$$

For $V = 20{,}000\text{ cm}^3$ and $\alpha = 25^\circ$:
- Height $h \approx 44.5\text{ cm}$.
- Top internal diameter $D = 2 \cdot h \cdot \tan(\alpha) \approx 41.5\text{ cm}$.
- Liquid level at 50% fill ($0.7937 \cdot h$) sits at $35.3\text{ cm}$ above apex. Freeboard equals $9.2\text{ cm}$.
- Cone lateral surface area:

$$A_{\text{lat}} = \frac{\pi \cdot R^2}{\sin(\alpha)} = \frac{\pi \cdot (20.75)^2}{\sin(25^\circ)} \approx 0.32\text{ m}^2$$

This $0.32\text{ m}^2$ lateral area matches the required jacket heat-transfer area calculated independently in Chapter 11 ($0.32\text{ m}^2$ at $U = 100\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$ and $\Delta T = 20\text{ K}$).

## 1b. Top closure and bottom apex: head geometry selection

![Comparison of Vessel Head Profiles: Flat vs Torispherical vs Ellipsoidal](/diagrams/head_type_comparison.png)

### Comparing head types for vacuum and pressure service

| Head Type | Geometry Ratios | External Pressure (Vacuum) Behavior | Application Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat Cover** | Simple plate | Resists load by bending only. Requires thick, heavy plate as diameter grows. | Avoid on vessels above 200 mm diameter. Impractical thickness under vacuum. |
| **Torispherical (ASME F&D)** | Crown radius $L \approx D$, knuckle $r \ge 0.06D$. Knuckle factor $M = \frac{3+\sqrt{L/r}}{4} \approx 1.77$. | Equivalent sphere radius $R_o = L \approx D$ under ASME UG-33. | **Practical default.** Readily stocked, economical to form. Knuckle thickness penalty is minimal at modest diameters. |
| **2:1 Ellipsoidal** | Crown radius $L = 0.9D$, knuckle $r = 0.17D$. Depth equals $D/4$. | Equivalent sphere radius $R_o = 0.9D$ under ASME UG-33. Continuous curvature eliminates stress concentrations. | Premium upgrade. Provides lower wall thickness and higher buckling resistance. Tooling costs are higher. |

### Dual load case verification

You must evaluate two distinct load cases:
1. **Full vacuum (external pressure, ASME UG-33)**: Governs baseline wall thickness. Evaluated using the equivalent sphere radius $R_o$.
2. **Steam sterilization (internal pressure, ASME UG-32)**: Clean steam at 2 to 4 barg.
   - For 2:1 Ellipsoidal: $t = \frac{P \cdot D \cdot K}{2 \cdot S \cdot E - 0.2 \cdot P}$ with $K = 1.0$.
   - For Torispherical: $t = \frac{P \cdot L \cdot M}{2 \cdot S \cdot E - 0.2 \cdot P}$ with $M \approx 1.77$.

Run both checks. External vacuum governs minimum thickness in almost all conical freeze dryers.

### Bottom apex transition

The conical shell must not terminate in a sharp point. Truncate the apex into a cylindrical nozzle stub sized for your bottom discharge valve (DN100 to DN150, per file 24). Form a knuckle radius at the cone-to-cylinder transition to mitigate bending stresses per ASME Section VIII Appendix 1.

## 3. Agitator Kinematics & Mixing Dynamics

![Hosokawa Nauta Three-Action Conical Mixing Dynamics](/diagrams/nauta_three_action_mixing_dynamics.png)

![Nauta Agitator Screw, Flush Ball Valve & Wall Clearance Anatomy](/photos/nauta_internal_components_auger_valves_clearance.png)

### Three-Action Triad Kinematics
1. **Vertical Wall Conveyance**: High-speed auger rotation lifts product upwards in a thin spiral layer along the heated vessel wall.
2. **$360^\circ$ Orbital Precession**: The top orbit arm sweeps the auger along the conical periphery at 1.0–2.5 RPM, distributing material circumferentially.
3. **Core Gravitational Cascade**: Lifted product discharges off the top flighting into the vessel center, cascading down the central void to the apex.

### Agitator Comparison: Screw vs. Ribocone Ribbons
- **Cantilevered Conical Screw (Nauta)**: Optimal for low-shear powder turnover and freeze drying. Operates with low Froude numbers ($Fr = \frac{\omega^2 r}{g} < 0.1$), preserving fragile crystals, living bacterial cultures, and delicate API matrices without attrition.
- **Single Ribbon / Double Ribbon (Ribocone)**: Central spiral ribbon sweep suited for viscous pastes and high-solids damp cakes. Higher mechanical shear and larger radial drag force.

### Sizing Kinematics: The 10:1 Power Ratio
- **Work Distribution**: The screw auger performs **over 90% of the mechanical displacement work** inside the cone, while the orbit arm merely guides the auger trajectory.
- **Motor Sizing Rule**: Maintain an approximate **10:1 auger-to-orbit power ratio**:
  - Screw Drive Motor: 1.5–2.2 kW (at 60–120 RPM, high breakaway torque for frozen granules).
  - Orbital Arm Motor: 0.25–0.37 kW (at 1.0–2.5 RPM).
- **Wall Clearance**: Precision tolerance of **3.0 to 5.0 mm** between auger flighting edge and the inner cone wall. Prevents thermal crust buildup while guaranteeing zero metal-to-metal contact during thermal expansion.

### Cantilevered Suspension vs. Bottom Bearing
- **Pharmaceutical Prohibition**: Bottom pintle bearings are strictly unacceptable in active freeze dryers. Submerged bottom bearings trap product fines, harbor cleaning residues (failing CIP swab tests), and risk lubricant leaching into sterile batches.
- **Cantilevered Top Suspension**: Agitator screw is suspended entirely from the orbital arm universal joint in the top dome. Eliminates 100% of submerged dynamic seals.

### Jacket construction options

![Nauta Conical Vessel with External Spiral Half-Pipe Heating Jacket](/photos/nauta_model_44605_spiral_jacket.png)

Three jacket configurations suit the conical body:
- **Plain annular jacket**: Simple fabrication. Yields lower heat-transfer fluid velocities and lower pressure ratings.
- **Half-pipe coil jacket**: Welded semicircular pipe wrapped helically around the cone. Supports 6 to 10 bar fluid circulation from high-flow TCUs. Provides uniform velocity and heat transfer.
- **Dimple jacket**: Spot-welded sheet with hydraulic expansion. Light weight, suitable for moderate circulation pressures.

For temperature cycling between -55 °C and +80 °C, half-pipe coil jackets offer optimal structural rigidity and heat-transfer performance.

## 4. Materials of construction

- **316L Stainless Steel (UNS S31603)**: Required for all product-contact surfaces. Resists corrosive CIP chemicals and provides cryogenic toughness. Exterior structural members use 304 stainless steel.
- **ASME BPE Sanitary Finishes**: Surface finish dictates cleaning efficacy.

| BPE Designation | Maximum $R_a$ ($\mu\text{m}$) | Maximum $R_a$ ($\mu\text{in}$) | Surface Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| SF1 | 0.51 | 20 | Mechanical Polish |
| SF2 | 0.64 | 25 | Mechanical Polish |
| SF3 | 0.76 | 30 | Mechanical Polish |
| **SF4** | **0.38** | **15** | **Electropolished** |
| SF5 | 0.50 | 20 | Electropolished |

Specify **SF4 ($R_a \le 0.38\,\mu\text{m}$, electropolished)** for all internal product-contact walls. Electropolishing removes micro-roughness and enriches surface chromium content.
- **Chemical Passivation**: Perform acid passivation per ASTM A967 or ASTM B912 following welding and polishing.
- **Elastomer Gaskets**: Use steam-resistant EPDM or PTFE-sheathed fluoroelastomers meeting USP Class VI and FDA 21 CFR 177.2600.

### ASME Section VIII and ASME BPE are complementary

ASME Section VIII governs mechanical pressure containment, calculating shell thickness to resist buckling and rupture. ASME BPE governs hygienic integrity, defining radius geometries, weld profiles, drainability, and surface roughness. Apply both standards simultaneously to the vessel.

## 5. Pressure and vacuum vessel design

The vessel operates under full external vacuum (1.013 bar differential):
- Thin conical shells fail through **elastic buckling** before reaching material yield stress.
- Design to **ASME BPVC Section VIII, Division 1, UG-28 through UG-30**. UG-28 specifies external pressure thickness calculations with a minimum 3:1 safety margin against elastic collapse.
- In Europe, design to **EN 13445** under the **Pressure Equipment Directive (PED 2014/68/EU)**.
- Jacket shells experience simultaneous internal fluid pressure and external vacuum. Verify inner cone stability under combined loads.
- Verify cryogenic impact toughness per ASME UCS-66 for design metal temperatures down to -55 °C.

## 6. Bottom discharge valve

Hosokawa utilizes a sanitary **ball-segment valve** (such as the ISEM series). The spherical segment closure sits flush with the internal cone contour. This design eliminates dead legs and prevents unmixed product pockets. Sized for DN100 to DN150 clamp flanges.
