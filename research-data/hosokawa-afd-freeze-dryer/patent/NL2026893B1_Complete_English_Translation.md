## PART 1: COMPLETE ENGLISH DESCRIPTION

### Background of the Invention
The invention relates to a freeze-dryer with a freeze-drying chamber having an inlet for allowing material to be dried to be introduced into the freeze-drying chamber and configured for being evacuated at least partially, and a vacuum source in fluid communication with the drying chamber and configured for evacuating the freeze-drying chamber at least partially, wherein a vacuum fluid path is defined between the freeze-drying chamber and the vacuum source.

Such a freeze-dryer is for example known from EP 1 601 919 B1 disclosing a method and device for freeze-drying solutions and liquid containing solid substances, in which a vessel is applied having a downwardly conical shape with inside a rotating mixing member that moves with a small interspace along the wall of the vessel.

### Summary of the Invention
A disadvantage of the known freeze-dryer may be that the part of the material to be dried that is dried already remains within the freeze-drying chamber while drying the remaining of the material to be dried. Therefore, all product is continuously worked during the entire duration of the drying process through the action of the rotating mixing member. This is disadvantageous for applications where sensitivity to shear is of critical importance. For example, in the freeze drying of probiotics and other living cells or in the freeze drying of spherical PLGA-based formulations.

Another disadvantage may be that the available heat transfer area is used inefficiently, due to decreasing product volume as the freeze-drying progresses.

It is an object of the present invention to ameliorate or to eliminate one or more disadvantages of the known freeze-dryer, or to at least provide an alternative freeze-dryer.

According to a first aspect, the invention provides a freeze dryer, comprising:
a freeze-drying chamber having an inlet for allowing material to be dried to be introduced into the freeze-drying chamber and configured for being evacuated at least partially; and
a vacuum source in fluid communication with the drying chamber and configured for evacuating the freeze-drying chamber at least partially, wherein a vacuum fluid path is defined between the freeze-drying chamber and the vacuum source;
wherein the freeze dryer further comprises a material collector configured for collecting dried material from the freeze-drying chamber, which material collector filter is arranged outside the freeze-drying chamber and within the vacuum fluid path between the freeze-drying chamber and the vacuum source.

During use, the vacuum source evacuates the freeze-drying chamber at least partially and during freezing of the initial material to be dried. Once pressure is low enough, the ice particles formed begin to sublime. As sublimation progresses, a vapor flow is present between the freeze-drying chamber and the vacuum source, passing the material collector. While the vapor flows towards the vacuum source, the individual ice particles shrink in size, resulting in the release of a powdery dust of the material. The powdery dust flows towards the material collector, where the powdery dust is collected from the vapor flow. As a result, the vapor flow entering the vacuum source is at least partially free of dried material, and dried material is removed from the freeze-drying chamber during the freeze drying process.

Because the dried material is removed from the freeze-drying chamber during the freeze-drying process, the dried material is no longer subjected to the mechanical agitation occurring in the freeze-drying chamber. This is particularly advantageous for shear-sensitive materials such as living organisms (e.g. bacteria, enzymes) or spherical drug particles where mechanical shear degrades viability or alters surface morphology.

Furthermore, removing dried material from the chamber maintains a relatively high concentration of frozen material per unit of contact surface area inside the chamber, thereby optimizing heat transfer through the vessel wall throughout the entire drying cycle.

In an embodiment, the material collector is arranged adjacent to and/or beside the freeze-drying chamber. This permits installation in facilities with standard ceiling heights, avoiding excessive vertical stack-up.

In an embodiment, the material collector comprises a collector housing having a vacuum outlet in fluid communication with the vacuum source and an inlet in fluid communication with the freeze-drying chamber, defining a collector fluid path. A collecting filter screen divides the collector path into an upstream path section and a downstream clean path section.

In an embodiment, the collector housing comprises a material outlet, preferably at or near the bottom of the collector housing, configured to discharge the collected dried material.

In an embodiment, the collector housing comprises a double-walled jacket configured to receive a heating fluid. This heating jacket prevents subliming water vapor from condensing or freezing onto the filter candle surfaces, maintaining powder dryness.

In an embodiment, the freeze dryer comprises a receiver for collected material, arranged at or near the material collector. The receiver comprises a receiver chamber configured to receive and hold collected powder under sterile vacuum conditions. The receiver may also include a double-walled heating jacket to perform secondary drying on the collected powder.

In an embodiment, the material collector is arranged at a first height above the freeze-drying chamber. An isolation valve is arranged within the vacuum fluid path between the freeze-drying chamber and the material collector. The valve can be moved between an open position, a closed position, and an intermediate restricted position.

In an embodiment, a bypass conduit is provided between the freeze-drying chamber and the material collector, bypassing the main isolation valve. During early freezing and initial sublimation, the main valve is open, allowing large ice crystals to fall back gravitationally into the chamber. As sublimation progresses, the valve is closed, forcing vapor and fine dust through the higher-velocity bypass conduit into the collector.

In an embodiment, a reverse pulse purging inlet is provided upstream of the filter candle, connected to an inert gas source (such as dry nitrogen) to provide high-pressure blow pulses that dislodge filter cake from the filter candle.

In an embodiment, the freeze-drying chamber is formed within a substantially downwardly conical vessel provided with a rotating helical mixing screw orbiting along the vessel inner wall. The vessel cover includes gas sweep nozzles oriented into the chamber to prevent powder ring formation and direct dust toward the vacuum outlet.
