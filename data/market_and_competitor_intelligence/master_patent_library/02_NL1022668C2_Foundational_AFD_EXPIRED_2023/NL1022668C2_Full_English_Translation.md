### Abstract
The invention relates to a method and a device for freeze-drying solutions and liquid-containing solid substances. According to the invention, the product is introduced into a conical vessel having a cooling/heating jacket and an agitating member (orbital mixing screw) that rotates along its own longitudinal axis while simultaneously orbiting along the inner vessel wall. The product is frozen dynamically into loose spherical ice granules, after which deep vacuum is drawn and sublimation heat is supplied via the heated wall jacket. The continuous boundary layer renewal prevents the formation of an insulative dry product cake, increasing the effective heat transfer coefficient to 65-75 W/(m^2*K) and reducing batch cycle times by up to 80%.

---

### Background of the Invention & Prior Art Bottleneck
Freeze drying (lyophilization) is a well-known preservation process for heat-sensitive active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), biologics, and food products. In conventional freeze drying, the product is frozen statically on flat trays or shelves inside a vacuum cabinet. 

A severe disadvantage of conventional shelf freeze dryers is the relatively low heat transfer rate during primary drying. As sublimation progresses from the upper surface downward, a porous layer of already-dried product forms above the frozen core. This dry layer has an extremely low thermal conductivity (k = 0.02 - 0.04 W/(m*K)) and acts as a **thermal insulator / thermal resistance barrier**. Heat supplied by the shelves or radiation cannot effectively penetrate through this insulative dry layer to reach the receding ice sublimation front without risk of scorching or collapsing the dry product cake. Consequently, conventional shelf lyophilization requires **48 to 72 hours per batch**. Furthermore, the resulting product forms a hard cake that requires manual harvesting and crushing.

---

### The Invention & Physical Mechanism
The object of the invention is to provide a method and device that substantially eliminates the thermal insulator problem, dramatically shortens freeze-drying time, and directly yields a uniform, free-flowing powder.

This object is achieved by conducting the freezing, sublimation, and desorption steps within a downwardly conical vessel provided with an agitating screw orbiting adjacent to the conical vessel wall:
1. **Dynamic Inoculation & Granulation**: Liquid solution is sprayed or poured into the vessel while cooling medium (-40°C to -50°C) is circulated through the double jacket. The orbiting mixing screw keeps the liquid in continuous motion, converting it into free-flowing, frozen spherical pellets (100 µm to 800 µm) rather than a solid frozen cake.
2. **Boundary Layer Scraping**: Deep vacuum (0.05 to 0.5 mbar) is applied to initiate sublimation. Heating medium (+20°C to +80°C) is introduced into the jacket. The orbiting screw continuously scrapes the boundary layer off the conical wall, throwing fresh frozen particles into direct contact with the heated wall.
3. **Multiplied Heat Flux**: The overall heat transfer coefficient increases from 5-10 W/(m^2*K) to **65-75 W/(m^2*K)**. The primary drying stage is completed within **6 to 10 hours**.
4. **Direct Bottom Discharge**: The final product is discharged as a loose, uniform, dry powder through a flush sanitary bottom ball segment valve without any cake crushing or manual harvesting.

### Complete English Translation of Claims (Claims 1 to 22)

1. **Claim 1 (Method)**: Method for freeze-drying solutions and liquid-containing solid substances, characterized in that a vessel is applied having a downwardly conical shape with inside a rotating mixing member that moves with a small interspace along the wall of the vessel, wherein the product to be dried is introduced into the vessel and is frozen therein, after which the vessel is evacuated and heat is supplied via the wall of the vessel while the mixing member is driven in rotation.
2. **Claim 2**: Method according to claim 1, characterized in that the product is frozen by cooling the wall of the vessel.
3. **Claim 3**: Method according to claim 1 or 2, characterized in that the mixing member is rotated during freezing of the product.
4. **Claim 4**: Method according to claim 3, characterized in that the product is converted into a granular frozen mass during freezing.
5. **Claim 5**: Method according to any one of the preceding claims, characterized in that the vessel is evacuated to an operating pressure below the triple point of the solvent present in the product.
6. **Claim 6**: Method according to claim 5, characterized in that the vessel is evacuated to an operating pressure between 0.01 mbar and 5 mbar, preferably between 0.05 mbar and 1 mbar.
7. **Claim 7**: Method according to any one of the preceding claims, characterized in that heat is supplied through the wall of the vessel by circulating a heating medium through a jacket surrounding the conical wall.
8. **Claim 8**: Method according to any one of the preceding claims, characterized in that the mixing member comprises an agitating screw extending parallel to the conical vessel wall and driven to rotate about its own longitudinal axis while orbiting around the central axis of the vessel.
9. **Claim 9**: Method according to claim 8, characterized in that the agitating screw lifts product along the conical wall, after which the product moves downward through the central core of the vessel.
10. **Claim 10**: Method according to any one of the preceding claims, characterized in that sublimated vapor is discharged from the vessel through a vapor outlet provided with a dust filter candle.
11. **Claim 11**: Method according to claim 10, characterized in that the dust filter candle is periodically purged by reverse gas pulses.
12. **Claim 12**: Method according to any one of the preceding claims, characterized in that the dried product is discharged through an outlet opening at the bottom apex of the conical vessel.
13. **Claim 13 (Apparatus)**: Device for freeze-drying solutions and liquid-containing solid substances, comprising a vessel having a downwardly conical shape, a jacket surrounding the conical wall of the vessel for circulating a cooling medium and a heating medium, an agitating member arranged within the vessel and movable with a small interspace along the wall of the vessel, a drive for rotating the agitating member, a vacuum connection for evacuating the vessel, and a product discharge opening at the bottom of the vessel.
14. **Claim 14**: Device according to claim 13, characterized in that the agitating member comprises a mixing screw.
15. **Claim 15**: Device according to claim 14, characterized in that the mixing screw is suspended as a cantilever from an orbiting swivel arm positioned at the top of the vessel.
16. **Claim 16**: Device according to claim 15, characterized in that the vessel is free of any bottom bearing or bottom shaft guide for the mixing screw.
17. **Claim 17**: Device according to any one of claims 13-16, characterized in that the interspace between the mixing member and the vessel wall is between 2 mm and 10 mm, preferably between 3 mm and 5 mm.
18. **Claim 18**: Device according to any one of claims 13-17, characterized in that the top of the vessel is sealed with a cover dome provided with a sintered porous filter assembly.
19. **Claim 19**: Device according to claim 18, characterized in that the filter assembly comprises reverse pulse blowback nozzles connected to a pressurized inert gas source.
20. **Claim 20**: Device according to any one of claims 13-19, characterized in that the bottom discharge opening is provided with a flush-sealing spherical segment valve.
21. **Claim 21**: Device according to any one of claims 13-20, characterized in that the device further comprises Clean-In-Place (CIP) rotary spray heads.
